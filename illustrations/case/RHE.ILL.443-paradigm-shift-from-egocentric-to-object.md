# [RHE.ILL.443] Paradigm shift from egocentric to objective spatial model; historical precedent for the same intellectual move in temporal thinking

## Source

In early astronomy, people assumed that because they see the earth standing still and 
things moving in the skies, the earth must be standing still and the planets and stars 
moving. When Copernicus suggested the earth was just another planet moving around the 
sun, he was suggesting a less egocentric view of the cosmos, one in which humans lost 
their special position at the centre of the universe.

## Structural Core

Observational egocentrism (appearance from observer position) mistaken for ontology; Copernicus overcame this by treating Earth as one planet among others

## Boundaries (breaks_when)

When the egocentric frame is actually the correct reference frame (e.g., in local physics, heliocentric frame is not inherently more 'objective')
