# [RHE.ILL.002] Why whole-part and class-member relations must be logically distinct; demonstrates that whole-part cannot govern membership in abstract collectives

## Source

Consider a person's hand, it is undoubtedly part of the person. Therefore, if the person
is part of the group, his or her hand would also be part of the group. However, the
person's hand is clearly not part of the group, so the person's connection to the group
cannot be whole–part.

## Structural Core

If whole-part relation were transitive (A part of B, B part of C → A part of C), then a person's hand would be part of a social group; this fails empirically and logically, disproving whole-part as the connecting relation

## Boundaries (breaks_when)

When objects are concrete mereological wholes without abstract class membership, or when transitivity of composition is enforced
