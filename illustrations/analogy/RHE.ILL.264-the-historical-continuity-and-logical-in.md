# [RHE.ILL.264] The historical continuity and logical inevitability of the file-record paradigm; digital systems inherited conceptual structure from physical precedent

## Source

The terms 'file' and 'record' are rooted in paper and ink technology. Well before the 
invention of computer technology, records were made on pieces of paper and kept in files. 
These paper records and files were based on the way in which information is naturally stored on paper.

## Structural Core

Digital file-record systems correspond to paper-based file-record systems because both organize information hierarchically (information → record → file) to enable retrieval

## Boundaries (breaks_when)

When information volume exceeds human manual capacity, when real-time indexing is required, or when simultaneous multi-user access is needed—digital systems enable these scenarios in ways paper systems fundamentally cannot
