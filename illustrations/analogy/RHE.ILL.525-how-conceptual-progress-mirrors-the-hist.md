# [RHE.ILL.003] How conceptual progress mirrors the historical development of increasingly accurate categorical distinctions

## Source

We saw how certain pre-logical cultures thought corn was the same as deer.
Similarly, some people quite happily talk and think of people as part of groups in the
'same' way as they think of their finger as part of their hand. They have not yet
acquired the logical paradigm's more accurate way of seeing whole–part patterns.

## Structural Core

Pre-logical inability to distinguish between ontologically different categories (ancient perception) parallels modern linguistic/conceptual imprecision in distinguishing whole-part from class-member relations

## Boundaries (breaks_when)

When speakers are consciously aware of the distinction but choose to ignore it for pragmatic communicative reasons
