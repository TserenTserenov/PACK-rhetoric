# [RHE.ILL.453] The defining mereological properties that distinguish stuff from things; category membership can be determined through composition/decomposition testing rather than mere linguistic convention

## Source

"If we put two bits of stuff together, then we have one bigger bit of stuff. 
If we divide a bit of stuff in two, then we have two smaller bits of stuff. 
Whereas, if we divide a thing, such as a car, in two, then all we get is two 
worthless pieces of junk."

## Structural Core

Operational tests (composition and decomposition operations) expose fundamental logical difference: stuff exhibits closure under composition and decomposition (stuff+stuff=stuff, stuff÷2=stuff), while things do not (thing÷2≠thing but rather thing÷2=junk)

## Boundaries (breaks_when)

when composite things are analyzed at sufficient granularity that they become mass-noun-like (e.g., car→atoms→particles), revealing that stuff/thing categorization depends on analytical level rather than being absolute
